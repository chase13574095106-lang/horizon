## [Google Research's Cogentic Multi-Agent System Discovers New Math Proofs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40324v1) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Google Research introduced Cogentic, a multi-agent system built on Gemini that uses a prove-verify loop with adversarial verification to automatically discover mathematical proofs. It produced new results on 5 open problems in online learning, auction theory, and mechanism design, all independently verified by domain experts. This marks a significant advancement in AI-driven mathematical discovery, showing that multi-agent coordination can tackle genuinely open research problems without expert hints. It could accelerate progress in theoretical computer science and economics, and signals a new paradigm for AI-assisted research. Cogentic lets multiple independent provers explore different directions while a dedicated component performs adversarial verification, storing confirmed results in a persistent verification ledger. The system works from the problem statement alone, with no expert hints, and the new results are detailed in accompanying papers.

## [2025 Nobel Prize in Medicine Awarded for Peripheral Immune Tolerance Discoveries](https://t.me/zaihuapd/44174) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The 2025 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine was awarded to Mary E. Brunkow, Fred Ramsdell, and Shimon Sakaguchi for their pioneering discoveries in peripheral immune tolerance. Their work identified key mechanisms, including regulatory T cells and the FOXP3 gene, that prevent the immune system from attacking the body's own tissues. This recognition highlights foundational advances in immunology that explain how the body avoids autoimmune diseases, with broad implications for developing therapies for autoimmune disorders, cancer, and organ transplantation. It underscores the growing importance of immune regulation in modern medicine. The awarded research focuses on peripheral tolerance, which operates after T and B cells leave the thymus and bone marrow, and on regulatory T cells (Tregs) that express CD4, FOXP3, and CD25. Tregs suppress effector T cell activity, and their dysregulation is linked to autoimmune diseases and cancer progression.

telegram · zaihuapd · Oct 2, 14:15

**Background**: The immune system must distinguish between the body's own cells and foreign threats; when this fails, autoimmune diseases occur. Central tolerance eliminates self-reactive cells in the thymus and bone marrow, but some escape, making peripheral tolerance essential. Peripheral tolerance, mediated by mechanisms like regulatory T cells, suppresses these self-reactive cells in other tissues. The FOXP3 gene is a master regulator of Treg development and function, and mutations in it cause severe autoimmune disorders.

## [AI Ataraxos Beats Top Human Stratego Player on a Budget](https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/10/ai-finally-beat-the-best-stratego-player-in-history-and-did-it-on-a-budget/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Researchers introduced Ataraxos, an AI system that defeated Pim Niemeijer, a four-time world champion Stratego player, 15 games to one with four draws in a 20-game series. Ataraxos achieved this while playing roughly 34 times fewer games than DeepMind's DeepNash, demonstrating dramatically better sample efficiency. This marks a major milestone in AI for imperfect-information games, a class of problems where the best move depends on information the player cannot see. The techniques developed could eventually help humans make strategic decisions in real-world situations where some information is hidden, such as negotiations or military planning. Ataraxos was built using general techniques for self-play reinforcement learning and test-time search under massive amounts of hidden information, and it outperformed more expensive models. The research was published in Nature with an accompanying arXiv paper (2511.07312).

**Background**: Stratego is a board wargame where players cannot see the identities of their opponent's pieces, making it a game of imperfect or hidden information. Unlike chess or Go, where all pieces are visible, hidden-information games are much harder for AI because the optimal move depends on unknown factors. DeepMind's DeepNash was a previous milestone in 2022, but it required enormous computational resources and many more training games.

## [Greg Kroah-Hartman Debunks Mythos LLM's 79 Kernel Vulnerabilities](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NnV_cWeoo5Q) ⭐️ 8.0/10

At Kernel Recipes 2026, Linux kernel maintainer Greg Kroah-Hartman presented a detailed breakdown of the 79 kernel vulnerabilities reportedly found by Anthropic's Mythos LLM, showing that only about 20 required fixes, while 24 had no detail, 14 were not bugs, 3 were fabricated, and 15 were already fixed in the latest release. He noted that the entire set of valid findings amounted to roughly one hour of kernel development work. This data-driven critique challenges the hype around LLMs in security and raises questions about AI safety marketing, especially when companies claim their models are too dangerous to release while also promoting vulnerability-discovery capabilities. It matters to open-source maintainers, security researchers, and anyone evaluating AI-generated bug reports. Of the 20 fixes needed, 7 assumed a malicious filesystem image and 2 assumed an attacker could influence other inputs, meaning many findings relied on unrealistic threat models. Kroah-Hartman also noted that Mythos essentially pattern-matched decades of prior kernel patches and applied them elsewhere, and Anthropic failed to credit the original kernel developers who fixed those CVEs.

## [Zig v0.17.0 Released With Faster Builds and Pragmatic LLM Stance](https://ziglang.org/download/0.17.0/release-notes.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Zig project released version 0.17.0, whose release notes highlight substantial progress toward language stabilization, a reworked build system, and notably faster incremental compilation, especially for x86_64-linux projects. The release also signals a shift in the team's stance on large language models, moving from a hardline anti-LLM position toward a more pragmatic approach, including using LLMs to help discover bugs. Zig is one of the fastest-maturing systems programming languages and a potential long-term competitor to C, so each release toward 1.0 matters to developers building low-level and performance-critical software. The softened LLM stance also matters because it reflects a broader industry debate about how open-source language projects should handle AI-generated contributions. The release notes describe 0.17.0 as a key step toward stabilizing the language, a prerequisite before tagging Zig 1.0, with the build system overhaul and incremental compilation improvements being the most tangible user-facing changes. The LLM shift appears to be inspired partly by results from SQLite, with core developer Andrew Kelley warming to LLMs as a tool on the path to bug-free software.

**Background**: Zig is a general-purpose systems programming language and toolchain created by Andrew Kelley and first announced in 2016, designed as a general-purpose improvement to C with features like compile-time generics, manual memory management, and no macros or preprocessor. It is free and open-source under the MIT License, with development funded by the Zig Software Foundation through corporate sponsorships and donations. Because Zig has not yet reached 1.0, its language and standard library are still unstable, and its ecosystem remains relatively small compared to established languages.

## [arXiv Bans Authors One Year for Unchecked LLM Content](https://t.me/zaihuapd/44166) ⭐️ 8.0/10

arXiv has announced that authors who submit papers containing clearly unchecked LLM-generated content — such as hallucinated citations, leftover LLM meta-comments, or placeholder text like 'table data is only an example, please replace with real experimental data' — will be banned from submitting for one year. After the ban ends, their subsequent submissions must first be accepted by a credible peer-reviewed venue before they can be posted to arXiv. This policy sets a clear precedent for accountability in academic publishing, making authors explicitly responsible for all content regardless of how it was generated. It directly targets the growing flood of LLM-assisted submissions and could influence how other preprint servers and journals handle AI-generated material. The penalty applies specifically to content that proves the author did not check the LLM output, including hallucinated references and leftover template text. arXiv's code of conduct states that authorship implies full responsibility for the paper's content, and the additional requirement of prior peer-reviewed acceptance after the ban adds a second layer of scrutiny.

telegram · zaihuapd · Oct 2, 06:21

**Background**: arXiv is a widely used open-access repository for electronic preprints in physics, mathematics, computer science, and related fields; submissions are moderated but not peer reviewed. LLMs can 'hallucinate' — generate plausible but non-existent references, data, or text — and a rising number of papers have been found to contain such fabricated citations. The arXiv endorsement system already requires new submitters to be endorsed before posting in certain categories, and this new policy extends that gatekeeping logic to penalize demonstrably unchecked AI-generated content.
