## [Karpathy: The LLM Capability Perception Gap Is Widening Into a Sharp Funnel](https://twitter.com/karpathy/status/2109361546505966046) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Andrej Karpathy revived his April tweet to argue that the gap in shared understanding of LLM capability is widening, shifting from "two groups speaking past each other" into what he calls a sharp funnel. He estimates via napkin math that roughly 6 billion people (~75% of the population) have barely come in contact with LLMs at all. The observation crystallizes a growing divide between a small group of power users who see LLMs as transformative and the vast majority for whom AI has produced little tangible change, which shapes how the industry, policymakers, and the public debate AI's real-world value. It also fuels disputes over whether AI coding agents genuinely replace engineering work or merely shift the burden of review and maintenance. Karpathy frames the divide not as two camps debating each other but as a funnel, implying the gap may be structural rather than a matter of persuasion. Commenters push back on the 6B figure, noting that many people already consume LLM-generated content on social media without realizing it, so "contact" is hard to measure.

**Background**: Andrej Karpathy is a well-known AI researcher and educator, a founding member of OpenAI and former director of AI at Tesla, whose commentary on LLMs is widely followed. LLM stands for large language model, the technology behind tools like ChatGPT and Claude, and "agents" refer to systems that use such models to autonomously perform multi-step tasks such as writing code. The debate echoes earlier tech hype cycles, where promised transformations (taxis, crypto, drone delivery) were framed as world-changing before delivering unevenly.

## [Terence Tao's 'Math 2.0' Slides on AI's Impact on Mathematics](https://teorth.github.io/tao-web/slides/math-2-0-caltech-2026.pdf) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Terence Tao released a slide deck titled 'Math 2.0' (dated October 2026) arguing that AI has ended the era of scarce proofs and that mathematics must move beyond raw problem-solving to value exposition, community building, and new directions. The slides sparked a 309-point Hacker News discussion with 365 comments, including expert debate on AI's role in mathematics and medicine. As a Fields Medalist, Tao's vision could shape how the mathematical community adapts to AI, influencing research priorities, funding, and collaboration. The debate highlights broader tensions about AI's role in knowledge production and whether it should be a 'good citizen' in extending human understanding. Tao argues that 'open problems are lighthouses not destinations,' meaning they should guide theory extension rather than be mere targets, and that current math AI is not a 'good citizen' because it doesn't produce elegant additions to the theory codebase. The slides also include a medical analogy about AI-created drugs, which some commenters criticized as out of his lane.

**Background**: Terence Tao is a Fields Medalist and one of the world's leading mathematicians, known for work in harmonic analysis, PDEs, and combinatorics. 'Math 2.0' refers to his vision of a future where AI tools sustain the mathematical community and understanding, as opposed to 'Math 1.0' where proofs were scarce and problem-solving was central. The slides were presented at Caltech in 2026 and shared on his blog.

## [AI Vibe-Coded Browser Ports of Classic Games Surge, Run Smoothly](https://kotaku.com/we-might-be-cooked-as-these-vibe-coded-web-browser-ports-of-halo-the-simpsons-hit-and-run-and-gta-vice-city-seem-to-work-perfectly-2000743300) ⭐️ 8.0/10

In recent weeks, hundreds of AI vibe-coded decompilation projects have appeared, producing browser-based ports of games like Halo: Combat Evolved, Call of Duty: Black Ops, and GTA: Vice City that run near-perfectly, with one Halo port even supporting online multiplayer. Kotaku reports that Claude Opus 5.5 is extremely strong at decompilation, turning source-code reconstruction that once took months into a task that can be completed in a single overnight AI run. This marks a major shift in game reverse-engineering and preservation, as AI drastically lowers the time and skill barrier for porting classic games to the web. It also poses serious challenges for copyright enforcement, since publishers can send takedown notices but the ports are so fast and easy to produce that removal becomes a game of whack-a-mole. Most of the ported games are from the PS2/PS3 era, when the PS3 had only about 512 MB of RAM, while a browser alone now consumes roughly 1200 MB, so memory is no longer the bottleneck. The real change is that decompilation no longer requires months of human labor, and the resulting browser ports run smoothly and quickly.

telegram · zaihuapd · Oct 11, 03:10

**Background**: Vibe coding is an AI-assisted software development practice, coined by Andrej Karpathy in February 2025, in which a developer describes a task in natural language and a large language model generates the code, often without thorough review. Decompilation is the reverse process of compilation, reconstructing readable source code from a compiled binary, and it has traditionally been a slow, labor-intensive task for retro game preservation. Claude Opus 5.5 is Anthropic's flagship Opus-tier model in the Claude 5.5 generation, positioned as its most capable model for complex reasoning and coding tasks.

## [Japan Declares 'Cyberspace State of Emergency' After Surge in Attacks](https://www.ft.com/content/2d4333d1-8119-44a4-a5a5-2ee9ef2544fa?syn-25a6b1a6=1) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Japanese government has declared a 'cyberspace state of emergency' after more than 20 major companies across railways, automotive, hospitals, and government facilities were hit by cyberattacks in recent weeks, potentially exposing tens of millions of customer records. The Financial Services Agency ordered banks to stop accepting driver's licenses as identity proof for new accounts and instead require chip-enabled ID documents. This marks one of Japan's most severe cybersecurity crises, affecting critical infrastructure and financial services, and signals that the country's cyber defenses are widely considered inadequate. The policy shift on identity verification could reshape KYC practices across Japan's banking sector and set a precedent for other nations facing similar threats. JR East said its ticket booking system may have leaked 1.67 million customer email addresses, and multiple incidents have been linked to a ransomware attack on SoftBank subsidiary IDC Frontier, which disrupted IDCF Cloud and affected 495 companies and local governments. Experts note that AI is lowering the barrier for complex attacks, though there is no evidence yet linking the latest attacks to AI.

**Background**: Japan has historically lagged behind other advanced economies in cybersecurity readiness, with fragmented oversight and a shortage of skilled security professionals. Ransomware attacks have become increasingly common globally, targeting cloud providers and critical infrastructure to maximize disruption and extortion payouts. The 'cyberspace state of emergency' is an unusual government designation intended to mobilize resources and coordinate response across public and private sectors.
