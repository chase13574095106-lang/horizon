## [Huawei Unveils 'Tao's Law' to Redefine Semiconductor Scaling](https://t.me/zaihuapd/42966) ⭐️ 8.0/10

At the 2026 International Symposium on Circuits and Systems (ISCAS) held in Shanghai on May 25, 2026, Huawei officially proposed 'Tao's Law' (τ-law), which replaces traditional geometric scaling with 'temporal scaling' to continue semiconductor advancement. The company also announced that its new Kirin chip, set for release in autumn 2026, will be the first to fully adopt the logic folding technology. This marks the first time a Chinese company has proposed an industry-level evolution principle in the global semiconductor field, potentially offering an alternative path as Moore's Law approaches physical limits. If successful, it could reduce reliance on advanced lithography and reshape the competitive landscape of the semiconductor industry. Tao's Law focuses on systematically reducing the time constant (τ) through multi-level co-optimization across devices, circuits, chips, and systems, using logic folding as a core implementation technique. Huawei expects that by 2031, high-end chips based on this law will achieve a transistor density equivalent to that of the 1.4nm process node.

telegram · zaihuapd · Aug 4, 08:04

**Background**: Traditional semiconductor scaling, known as geometric scaling, follows Moore's Law by shrinking transistor dimensions to increase density and performance. However, as physical limits approach, this approach becomes increasingly difficult and costly. Tao's Law proposes an alternative: instead of shrinking geometries, it aims to reduce the time constant (τ) — the time a system takes to respond to signals — by redesigning circuit layouts and signal paths, thereby improving performance without requiring more advanced lithography.

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) has completed and submitted the mandatory national standard 'Safety Requirements for Autonomous Driving Systems of Intelligent Connected Vehicles' (GB 44721—2026) for approval, with public consultation starting June 17 and implementation scheduled for July 1, 2027. This is China's first mandatory standard specifically targeting L3 and L4 autonomous driving systems. This standard marks a shift from conceptual deregulation to hard safety constraints in China's autonomous driving regulation, establishing a unified safety baseline for L3/L4 systems. It will significantly impact automakers and technology companies by requiring rigorous safety case documentation and specific handling of human-machine handover and autonomous risk response, potentially influencing global industry practices. The standard introduces a Safety Case mechanism, requiring companies to systematically demonstrate safety using a 'claim-argument-evidence' structure. For L3, it emphasizes human-machine handover, including monitoring driver readiness and executing minimal-risk maneuvers if takeover fails; for L4, it focuses on the system's own risk-handling capabilities. The standard is designated GB 44721—2026.

**Background**: Autonomous driving levels are defined by SAE, with L3 (conditional automation) requiring the driver to take over upon request, while L4 (high automation) can handle all driving tasks within a specific Operational Design Domain (ODD) without driver intervention. Safety cases, as promoted by standards like UL 4600, have become a best practice for demonstrating AV safety. China's new standard aligns with this trend, mandating a structured safety argument for L3/L4 systems.
