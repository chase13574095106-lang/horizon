## [CRISPR Cas12a2 Shreds Cancer Cells Selectively](https://innovativegenomics.org/news/crispr-technique-selectively-shreds-cancer-cells/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Researchers led by Jennifer Doudna have developed a novel CRISPR-Cas12a2 approach that selectively shreds chromatin in cancer cells upon detecting tumor-specific mutations, effectively killing them while leaving healthy cells intact. The technique was published in Nature on June 8, 2026. This technique offers a potential treatment for previously 'undruggable' cancers, as it targets mutations rather than proteins. It represents a significant advance over earlier CRISPR-based cancer therapies by using Cas12a2's more destructive chromatin-shredding mechanism. Unlike Cas9, which only cuts DNA at the target site, Cas12a2 shreds the entire chromatin within the cell once activated by detecting a specific RNA sequence. The system is RNA-triggered, meaning it recognizes mutations at the RNA level rather than DNA.

hackernews · gmays · Jun 12, 15:15 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48505231)

**Background**: CRISPR-Cas systems are gene-editing tools derived from bacterial immune systems. Cas12a2 is a type V CRISPR nuclease that, upon binding a target RNA, non-specifically degrades single-stranded DNA and RNA, leading to chromatin shredding and cell death. This contrasts with Cas9, which makes a double-strand break at a specific DNA site.

## [WASI 0.3 Released with Component Model Shift](https://bytecodealliance.org/articles/WASI-0.3) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The Bytecode Alliance announced WASI 0.3, a new version of the WebAssembly System Interface that introduces significant changes from WASI 0.2, including a shift towards the component model. WASI 0.3 marks a major step in the evolution of WebAssembly beyond the browser, aiming to provide a secure, portable interface for applications across platforms. The component model direction could enable better interoperability and modularity, but has also sparked debate within the community. WASI 0.3 includes interface-level changes detailed in the release notes and an announcement post with examples. The release is based on the component model, which defines APIs for components rather than traditional Unix-like syscalls.

hackernews · mavdol04 · Jun 12, 13:51 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48504063)

**Background**: WebAssembly (Wasm) is a portable binary instruction format designed for high-performance execution in browsers and other environments. WASI (WebAssembly System Interface) provides a standardized set of APIs for Wasm programs to interact with the operating system, initially modeled after POSIX. The component model is a newer approach that defines interfaces between software components, aiming to improve modularity and security.

## [Preprint Alleges Huawei Pangu Copied Qwen Weights, New Detection Method](https://t.me/zaihuapd/41915) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A preprint paper by Zhang Ruichong from Tsinghua University proposes a new method called Matrix-Driven Instant Review (MDIR) to detect weight plagiarism in large language models, providing statistical evidence with extremely low p-values that suggests Huawei's Pangu model may have copied weights from Alibaba's Qwen model. This is significant because it raises serious plagiarism allegations against a major Chinese AI model (Huawei Pangu) and introduces a novel detection technique that could help ensure model integrity and fair competition in the AI industry. The MDIR method uses matrix analysis and large deviation theory to align and compare model embeddings and multi-layer weights, computing rigorous p-values, and can complete the analysis on a single PC within one hour. The method claims to accurately identify weight sources even after incremental pre-training, pruning, or permutation, while avoiding false positives.

telegram · zaihuapd · Jun 12, 08:07

**Background**: Large language models (LLMs) like Huawei's Pangu and Alibaba's Qwen are trained on massive datasets and their performance heavily depends on learned weights. Weight plagiarism occurs when one model's weights are copied from another without proper attribution, which violates open-source licenses and ethical norms. The MDIR method aims to provide a reliable way to detect such plagiarism.
