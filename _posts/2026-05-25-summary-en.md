California has proposed an amendment to exempt Linux from its upcoming age-verification law after backlash from the open-source community. ## [Huawei Proposes 'Tao's Law' for Semiconductor Scaling via Time](https://www.peopleapp.com/column/30052220655-500007509895) ⭐️ 8.0/10

At the 2026 IEEE International Symposium on Circuits and Systems (ISCAS) in Shanghai, Huawei unveiled 'Tao's Law' (τ-law), which replaces traditional geometric scaling with temporal scaling to advance semiconductor performance. Huawei claims to have designed and mass-produced 381 chips based on this principle over the past six years, and plans to launch a new Kirin chip using logic folding technology in autumn 2026. Tao's Law represents a potential paradigm shift in semiconductor scaling as Moore's Law approaches physical limits, offering a new path to continue improving transistor density and system performance. If validated, it could reduce the industry's reliance on extreme ultraviolet lithography and advanced process nodes, impacting global chip design and manufacturing strategies. The core of Tao's Law is to systematically reduce the time constant (τ) through techniques like logic folding and advanced packaging, rather than shrinking transistor physical dimensions. Huawei predicts that by 2031, high-end chips based on this law could achieve transistor density equivalent to that of a 1.4nm process.

telegram · zaihuapd · May 25, 01:35

**Background**: Traditional semiconductor scaling, known as 'geometric scaling' or 'More Moore,' has relied on shrinking transistor dimensions to increase density and performance, but this approach is hitting physical and economic limits. Tao's Law shifts the focus to reducing signal propagation delay (time constant) across device, circuit, chip, and system levels, enabling performance gains without requiring extreme lithography. The concept was detailed in a paper by Huawei's He Tingbo published on the Chinese Academy of Sciences preprint platform.

## [Semi-living human brains used for drug testing](https://www.science.org/content/article/not-alive-not-dead-disembodied-human-brains-used-drug-testing) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A study using partially revived human brains, via the BrainEx perfusion system, has been conducted for drug testing on neurological diseases like Alzheimer's and Parkinson's, raising ethical questions about the boundary between life and death. This approach could overcome limitations of animal models and improve drug development success rates for neurological disorders, but it challenges existing ethical frameworks regarding consciousness, personhood, and the definition of death. The brains, obtained from deceased donors, showed restored metabolic and cellular activity but no signs of consciousness or full neural activity; researchers emphasize they are not alive nor dead.

telegram · zaihuapd · May 25, 14:57

**Background**: The BrainEx perfusion system is a technology that restores circulation and some cellular functions in post-mortem brains. Traditionally, drug testing for neurological diseases relies on animal models or cell cultures, which often fail to predict human responses. This study represents a novel step toward using human brain tissue directly, but it also reignites debates on when life ends and what ethical protections apply to such 'semi-living' tissue.
