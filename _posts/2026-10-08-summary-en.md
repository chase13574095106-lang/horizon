## [Margaret Hamilton, Apollo Software Pioneer, Dies](https://news.mit.edu/2026/margaret-hamilton-computing-pioneer-dies-1007) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Margaret Hamilton, the computing pioneer who led the MIT Instrumentation Laboratory team that developed the Apollo Guidance Computer's on-board flight software, has died, as reported by MIT News. She directed the Software Engineering Division and oversaw more than 400 people working on Apollo's software. Hamilton's work was foundational to the Apollo missions and to the modern discipline of software engineering, and her death marks the loss of a figure whose influence spans space exploration and computing culture. Her legacy continues to shape how software is engineered for safety-critical systems today. Hamilton led development of the on-board flight software for the Apollo Guidance Computer (AGC) used in both the Command Module and Lunar Module, and she is widely credited with coining the term "software engineer." A famous 1969 MIT photograph shows her standing beside listings of the software her teams produced.

**Background**: The Apollo Guidance Computer was a digital computer installed aboard each Apollo command module and lunar module, responsible for guidance, navigation, and control during missions. Hamilton's team at the MIT Instrumentation Laboratory, which contracted with NASA in 1961, wrote the software that helped land astronauts on the Moon, including the Apollo 11 mission in July 1969.

## [2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry Awarded to Kagan and Soai for Chirality Work](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2026/press-release/) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry was jointly awarded to Henri B. Kagan and Kenso Soai for their discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis. Their work provided key insights into how homochirality may have first emerged in the essential molecules of life. This recognition highlights fundamental breakthroughs in asymmetric catalysis that have far-reaching impacts on the pharmaceutical industry, where the chirality of drug molecules can dramatically affect their potency and safety. It also offers potential clues to one of biology's deepest mysteries: the origin of homochirality in life. Kagan is recognized for discovering non-linear effects in asymmetric catalysis, while Soai is known for the Soai reaction, a landmark example of asymmetric autocatalysis in which a chiral product catalyzes its own formation with high enantiomeric excess. Their combined work explains how a small initial chiral imbalance can be amplified to produce nearly pure enantiomers.

hackernews · sasvari · Oct 7, 09:51 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49990470)

**Background**: Chirality is the geometric property of a molecule that makes it non-superimposable on its mirror image, like left and right hands. In living organisms, most biological molecules exist in only one chiral form, a phenomenon called homochirality, but the reason for this preference has long been a mystery. Asymmetric autocatalysis is a reaction in which a chiral compound catalyzes its own production, potentially explaining how a tiny initial imbalance could be amplified into the homochirality seen in life.

## [Chrome Ships JPEG XL Support After Years of Reversal](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/jpeg-xl-in-chrome) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Google announced that Chrome is shipping JPEG XL support, reversing its earlier decision to remove the format from Chromium. Firefox is also planning stable support, meaning JPEG XL could soon move from Safari-only to majority browser coverage. Chrome's support removes the biggest obstacle that has held JPEG XL back on the web, since the most popular browser's lack of support severely limited its adoption. With Firefox following, JPEG XL could become a widely supported next-generation image format alongside AVIF. JPEG XL supports both lossy and lossless compression and can losslessly shrink existing JPEGs by about 20%, while offering transparency, HDR, progressive decoding, and animation. It is designed for efficient software encoding and decoding without hardware acceleration, though it may be less ideal under strong CPU constraints compared with AVIF in some lossy cases.

hackernews · AshleysBrain · Oct 7, 11:25 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49991227)

**Background**: JPEG XL (JXL) is a free, open image format defined by ISO/IEC 18181 and developed by the JPEG committee, Google, and Cloudinary as a successor to legacy JPEG. Google removed JPEG XL support from Chrome in October 2022 citing low interest, which also stalled Firefox's plans. The format competes with AVIF and WebP, with WebP leading on encoding speed and ecosystem maturity while JPEG XL and AVIF offer more advanced compression.

## [Paper Questions Fidelity of OpenAI's Lean Navier-Stokes Proof](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08144) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new arXiv paper (2610.08144) argues that the Lean formalization of OpenAI's claimed Navier-Stokes blow-up proof does not faithfully correspond to the original natural-language proof, suggesting the formalized theorem may be weaker than the prose argument. The paper specifically claims the Lean proof does not match the natural-language proof of finite-time blow-up of solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations. This challenges the validity of one of the highest-profile AI-assisted mathematical results, raising questions about whether LLM-generated formalizations can be trusted to capture the full strength of human-readable proofs. It affects the AI, formal verification, and mathematics communities, and could influence how future AI-generated proofs are validated. The critique focuses on the equivalence between the natural-language proof and the Lean proof, not necessarily on the correctness of the Lean proof itself; as commenters note, natural language is less precise than Lean, so multiple translations are possible, and the LLM may have produced a minimal formalization that satisfies the theorem without the stronger claims of the original argument.

**Background**: The Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem is one of the Clay Institute's Millennium Prize Problems, asking whether smooth solutions always exist in three dimensions. OpenAI published an AI-generated solution in September 2026, including a writeup and a Lean 4 formal proof, claiming finite-time blow-up for the equations. Lean is an interactive theorem prover used for formal verification, where proofs are checked by a computer to ensure logical correctness.
