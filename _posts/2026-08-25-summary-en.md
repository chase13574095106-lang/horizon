Firefox 157 will enable JPEG XL support by default on all platforms, marking a major step for the next-generation image format. This follows the earlier availability of JPEG XL in Firefox Nightly and beta channels. This is significant because JPEG XL offers superior compression efficiency and features compared to existing formats like JPEG and PNG, potentially improving web performance and user experience. With Firefox and Chromium both adopting Rust-based implementations, it could accelerate cross-browser adoption and influence the future of web image standards. JPEG XL is a free and open standard defined by ISO/IEC 18181, supporting lossy and lossless compression, wide color gamut, HDR, and high bit depth. Both Firefox and Chromium are using the Rust-based jxl-rs library, while Apple has shipped libjxl (C++) in its platforms.

**Background**: JPEG XL is an image format developed by the Joint Photographic Experts Group (JPEG), Google, and Cloudinary, designed to meet the needs of web image delivery and professional photography. It supports features like layer support, up to 4099 channels for selection masks and spot colors, and CMYK compatibility. The format aims to replace older formats with better compression and modern features.
