## [Delhi Slashes Electricity Losses from 50% to 5%](https://spectrum.ieee.org/delhi-electricity-loss) ⭐️ 8.0/10

An IEEE Spectrum article details how Delhi reduced its electricity losses from roughly 50 percent to about 5 percent, a dramatic turnaround for a major city grid. The improvement came through a combination of technical upgrades, anti-theft measures, and utility reform rather than any single fix. Cutting losses this sharply means more revenue for utilities, more reliable power for residents, and less need for new generation capacity, which matters for fast-growing cities across the developing world. It also shows that commercial losses like theft, not just technical problems, can be tackled at scale. The losses were driven largely by rampant electricity theft, with businesses, residents, and even utility employees illegally hooking into streetlights and distribution lines. The reform effort included insulating power lines and using loss-reduction targets as the basis for privatization bids and tariff determination.

hackernews · rbanffy · Sep 29, 12:43 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49892245)

**Background**: Electricity distribution losses, often measured as AT&C (Aggregate Technical and Commercial) losses, combine physical energy lost in transmission and distribution with commercial losses from theft, fraud, and billing errors. The World Bank estimates that distribution utilities lose about $96 billion in annual revenue from commercial losses, mostly in emerging economies. Delhi's reform is notable because it targeted both the technical and social dimensions of the problem.

## [Study Exposes Ad Trackers in Nine Conversational AI Agents](https://jorgegarciaherrero.com/wp-content/interactivos/20260916-Prompt-like-a-butterfly-sting-like-a-tracker-(clean).pdf) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new paper titled "Prompt like a Butterfly, Sting like a Tracker" presents a systematic privacy analysis of the web and mobile deployments of nine prominent conversational AI services, using static and dynamic analysis to detect third-party Advertising and Tracking Services (ATSes) and characterize their data flows. The accompanying Hacker News discussion (407 points, 129 comments) surfaced concrete leaks, including ChatGPT sending unfinished prompts to a `conversation/prepare` endpoint and Perplexity exposing full conversations via UUID-based URLs. The findings show that conversational AI services, which users often treat as private spaces for sensitive thoughts, are embedded with the same ad-tracking infrastructure found across the broader web, potentially exposing prompts, writing patterns, and conversation histories. This matters for hundreds of millions of chatbot users and adds pressure on AI vendors to reconcile monetization and investor demands with user privacy expectations. The analysis covers both web and mobile deployments and evaluates how consent choices, subscription tiers, and access-control mechanisms influence tracking behavior, meaning even paid users may not be fully shielded. Community reports add that ChatGPT's `conversation/prepare` endpoint receives partial prompts before the user hits send, and that Perplexity treats a UUID in the URL as sufficient privacy protection even though visiting the link reveals the entire conversation.

**Background**: Conversational AI agents are services like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity that let users interact with large language models through chat interfaces. Advertising and Tracking Services (ATSes) are third-party scripts and SDKs commonly embedded in websites and apps to profile user behavior for ad targeting. Prior privacy research, including a 2025 Stanford study, has already raised concerns that chatbot conversations may not be as private as users assume, and this paper extends that work with a systematic, multi-service technical audit.

## [Anthropic: GLM-5.3 and Claude Mythos Preview Cross Binary Exploitation Threshold](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/29/anthropic-frontier-red-team/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Anthropic's Frontier Red Team evaluated several models on 100 randomly selected tasks from its internal Binary Exploitation benchmark and found that GLM-5.3 achieved full control flow hijacks in 4% of trials, while Claude Mythos Preview did so in 6%. Earlier models such as Claude Opus 4.6 and GLM-5.2 succeeded in none of the trials, marking a clear capability threshold being crossed. This is a notable AI security milestone because it shows that frontier models are beginning to autonomously perform end-to-end binary exploitation, a task previously requiring skilled human researchers. It has direct implications for the cyber offense/defense balance, especially since GLM-5.3 is an open-weights model that malicious actors could modify to weaken safety refusals. The evaluation used 100 randomly selected tasks from Anthropic's internal Binary Exploitation benchmark, and the reported success rates are low in absolute terms (4% and 6%), so the models are far from reliable autonomous exploit development. According to the accompanying Telegram summary, GLM-5.3 succeeded in 50 of 410 ExploitBench attempts versus 56 for Claude Mythos Preview, and its safety guardrails could be bypassed by simple methods with a 64% to 100% success rate in simulated tests.

rss · Simon Willison · Sep 29, 22:20

**Background**: Binary exploitation is the practice of finding and abusing memory-corruption bugs in compiled programs to make them behave in ways the attacker wants. A full control flow hijack means the attacker gains control of the program's execution path, for example by overwriting a return address on the call stack so that the program jumps to attacker-chosen code, a technique classically associated with return-oriented programming (ROP). Anthropic's Frontier Red Team is a group that stress-tests frontier AI systems to assess their implications for cybersecurity, national security, and autonomous systems. GLM-5.3 is Z.ai's flagship open-weights model, built on the same base model as GLM-5.2 with gains coming entirely from post-training.

## [Cloudflare launches cf CLI for AI agents, covering 3,000+ API operations](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-cf-cli-launch/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Cloudflare has released the open beta of cf CLI, a command-line tool generated from its API schemas that covers over 3,000 Cloudflare API operations, compared to roughly 280 operations supported by the existing Wrangler tool. The tool defaults to JSON output and supports command search and guided discovery, explicitly designed so that both developers and AI agents can discover, execute, and process Cloudflare API operations. This represents a notable shift toward designing developer tooling specifically for AI agents, since CLI text-in/text-out interfaces are a natural fit for LLM-driven automation. If agents can manage Workers, Access, WAF, and even domain purchases through a single tool, it could reshape how infrastructure is provisioned and how agentic workflows integrate with cloud platforms. The cf CLI is generated directly from Cloudflare's OpenAPI schemas, which is how it achieves such broad coverage, and it is currently in open beta. Cloudflare gives examples such as an agent creating and deploying a Worker, monitoring services, configuring Access and WAF, and purchasing a domain, all through the same tool.

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 29, 13:46

**Background**: Cloudflare has published OpenAPI schemas for its API since 2022, which makes it possible to auto-generate clients and tools from a single source of truth. Wrangler is Cloudflare's existing CLI focused on the Workers developer platform, but it only covers a subset of the company's API surface. Command-line interfaces are increasingly seen as an ideal interface for AI agents because they operate purely on text, making them easy for language models to invoke and parse.
