## [First Complete Reading of a Herculaneum Scroll via AI](https://scrollprize.org/firstscroll) ⭐️ 9.0/10

For the first time, an entire Herculaneum scroll has been read continuously from end to end using AI and machine learning techniques, as announced by the Vesuvius Challenge team. This breakthrough unlocks ancient texts that were thought lost forever, potentially recovering lost works of Greek philosophy and literature, and demonstrates the power of AI in digital humanities. The scroll, carbonized by the eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 79 AD, was read using AI-based virtual unwrapping and ink detection, yielding about 22 columns of text reviewed by papyrologists.

hackernews · verditelabs · Jun 25, 15:48 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48675179)

**Background**: The Herculaneum papyri are over 1,800 carbonized scrolls discovered in the 18th century at the Villa of the Papyri. They are the only surviving library from antiquity, but reading them without damage has been extremely difficult. The Vesuvius Challenge, launched in 2023, offered prizes for using AI to read these scrolls.

## [IBM Debuts World's First Sub-1nm Chip Technology](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-06-25-ibm-debuts-worlds-first-sub-1-nanometer-chip-technology) ⭐️ 9.0/10

IBM announced the world's first sub-1 nanometer chip technology, featuring a 0.7nm (7 angstrom) node with a revolutionary NanoStack transistor architecture that packs nearly 100 billion transistors on a chip the size of a fingernail. This breakthrough demonstrates that transistor scaling can continue below 1nm, potentially enabling future processors with dramatically higher performance and energy efficiency, and it reinforces IBM's role as a key innovator in semiconductor technology despite having sold its fabs. The 0.7nm node achieves roughly double the transistor density of IBM's previous 2nm node, with up to 50% performance improvement or 70% energy efficiency gain. IBM has not yet announced a manufacturing partner for this technology, but has previously licensed chip technology to Samsung and Rapidus.

hackernews · porridgeraisin · Jun 25, 15:33 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48674967)

**Background**: Semiconductor process nodes have historically been named after the minimum feature size, but in recent years the node name (e.g., 7nm, 5nm) no longer corresponds to a physical dimension; instead it indicates a generation of manufacturing technology with improved density and performance. IBM sold its chip fabrication plants to GlobalFoundries in 2014 but continues to conduct semiconductor research and license its innovations.

## [UN Adopts First Global Technical Regulation for Autonomous Driving](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202606/25/WS6a3cf7e7a310986e2b461ebc.html) ⭐️ 9.0/10

The UN World Forum for Harmonization of Vehicle Regulations (WP.29) voted to adopt the first Global Technical Regulation (GTR) for Automated Driving Systems (ADS), co-led by China, the EU, the US, Canada, Japan, and the UK. This regulation establishes a unified global framework for ADS safety and lifecycle management, which will harmonize standards across major economies and accelerate the deployment of safe autonomous vehicles worldwide. The GTR covers core technical indicators, manufacturer safety management, safety documentation, test validation, and post-deployment safety, forming a comprehensive lifecycle regulatory framework. China contributed multiple technical proposals and test data, and its domestic mandatory standard aligns with the GTR, with further refinements for L3 and L4 automation.

telegram · zaihuapd · Jun 25, 16:03

**Background**: The UN WP.29 is the global body responsible for harmonizing vehicle regulations. The ADS GTR was developed under the 1998 Agreement and provides performance-based, technology-neutral safety requirements. It complements a parallel UN Regulation under the 1958 Agreement, which is binding for contracting parties.

## [Zig's new bitCast semantics and LLVM backend improvements](https://ziglang.org/devlog/2026/#2026-06-25) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Zig's @bitCast semantics have been updated to be endian-agnostic, meaning the operation now behaves identically on all targets regardless of endianness. Additionally, the LLVM backend has received improvements that enhance code generation for bit-packed data. This change simplifies bit-level manipulation in systems programming, making code more portable and reducing bugs related to endianness. It also improves the efficiency of handling bit-packed binary headers and similar data structures. Under the new semantics, bitcasting a [2]u8 to a u16 will always map the first array element to the 8 least significant bits, regardless of target endianness. The LLVM backend improvements include better handling of arbitrary-width integers and packed structs.

hackernews · kouosi · Jun 25, 14:19 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48673825)

**Background**: Zig is a systems programming language that emphasizes safety and performance. @bitCast is a built-in function that reinterprets the bits of a value as a different type, commonly used for low-level data manipulation. Previously, its behavior depended on the target's endianness, leading to non-portable code.

## [German Court Holds Google Liable for AI Overview Errors](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jun/25/ai-and-liability/#atom-everything) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A German court ruled that Google is directly liable for false statements generated by its AI Overviews, treating the AI output as Google's own words. Bruce Schneier argues that AI agents should be legally considered agents of their deployers, making companies responsible for AI mistakes just as they would be for human employees. This landmark ruling sets a precedent that companies cannot hide behind AI to avoid liability for errors, potentially reshaping corporate incentives around AI deployment. It could accelerate regulatory frameworks for AI accountability worldwide. The ruling overturns previous case law that shielded search engine operators from liability for third-party content, specifically because AI Overviews generate new content rather than merely indexing existing pages. Google has announced it will appeal the decision.

rss · Simon Willison · Jun 25, 22:28

**Background**: AI Overviews are Google's AI-generated summaries that appear at the top of search results, synthesizing information from multiple sources. Unlike traditional search results that link to external websites, these overviews can produce novel statements that may be inaccurate or fabricated, a phenomenon known as hallucination. The legal question is whether the company operating the AI should be liable for such outputs, similar to how publishers are liable for content they produce.

## [Google Play to Enable External Billing in US, UK, EEA](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/06/play-expanded-billing.html) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Starting June 30, Google Play will allow developers in the US, UK, and European Economic Area to offer third-party in-app billing or external web links for purchases, separating service fees from billing fees. This is Google's largest expansion of billing flexibility, giving developers more choice and potentially lower costs, while responding to regulatory pressure in key markets. For the first $1 million in annual revenue and auto-renewing subscriptions, the service fee is 10%; other transactions are categorized by new vs. existing installs. Using Google Play Billing incurs an additional 5% settlement fee, while alternative billing or external links do not.

telegram · zaihuapd · Jun 25, 02:33

**Background**: Previously, Google required most apps to use its own billing system, which charged a 15-30% commission. Regulatory scrutiny, especially from the EU's Digital Markets Act, pushed Google to offer more flexibility. This change builds on a pilot program and introduces a new fee structure.
